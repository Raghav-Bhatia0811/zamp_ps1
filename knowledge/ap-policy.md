# Halden Foods — Accounts Payable Policy

Version 3.2 · effective 1 April 2026 · owner: Finance Controller. Applies to every supplier invoice paid by Halden Foods Pvt Ltd, Pune.

## §1 Purpose and principles

### §1.1 Purpose
This policy sets out how supplier invoices are received, checked, approved and paid. Its aim is that every rupee paid is owed, paid once, paid to the right account, and backed by records an auditor can follow.

### §1.2 Automation and human judgement
Invoices may be read and checked by automated systems. Automated systems may approve an invoice for payment only when every control in this policy passes. Any exception goes to a named person, who records a reason before approving or rejecting. Software that reads documents (OCR or AI) may capture data but must not decide payments; decisions follow the written rules below.

## §2 Receiving invoices

### §2.1 Channels and formats
Invoices are accepted at the AP mailbox as PDF, as scanned images, or as clear photographs of a paper invoice. Invoices sent to individual employees must be forwarded to the AP mailbox unchanged. Faxes and phone photos are accepted only if they meet the legibility standard in §2.2.

### §2.2 Legibility standard
AP must be able to read, without guessing, the supplier name and GSTIN, the invoice number and date, every amount, and the bank details. If any of these cannot be read with confidence, AP must ask the supplier for a clear copy (ideally the original PDF) and must not infer or "fill in" values. A blurred, cut-off or low-resolution copy is returned to the supplier with a request for a clearer copy; it is not rejected and the supplier is not penalised.

### §2.3 Handwritten alterations
Printed invoices with handwritten changes to amounts, quantities, rates, dates or bank details are not accepted. The supplier must issue a corrected invoice or a credit note. Handwritten notes added by Halden staff (for example a GRN number or "received") are allowed but are not evidence of anything and do not change the invoice.

### §2.4 Stamps and markings
An invoice stamped "PAID", "CANCELLED" or "VOID" must be held and checked before payment: it may already have been settled through petty cash, a corporate card, or an earlier payment run. "RECEIVED", "ENTERED", "VERIFIED" and similar internal stamps have no effect on processing. A "DUPLICATE" or "COPY" marking is processed normally, but the duplicate-invoice control in §6.1 applies with extra care.

### §2.5 Signature
Paper and scanned tax invoices should carry the signature of the supplier or its authorised representative, or a digital signature (CGST Rules, rule 46). Computer-generated invoices and e-invoices with an IRN need no physical signature. A missing signature on a paper invoice is noted as a warning; it does not by itself stop payment.

## §3 Supplier master

### §3.1 Approved suppliers only
Payments are made only to suppliers that are onboarded and active in the supplier master. An invoice from an unknown or inactive supplier is rejected and routed to Procurement for onboarding.

### §3.2 Suspended suppliers
A supplier may be suspended for lapsed KYC, failed compliance checks or dispute. Invoices from suspended suppliers are rejected until the suspension is lifted.

### §3.3 GSTIN
The GSTIN printed on the invoice must equal the GSTIN in the supplier master. A mismatch is held for AP review because input tax credit depends on the GSTIN being correct.

## §4 Validating the invoice

### §4.1 Mandatory fields
An invoice must show the supplier name, an invoice number, an invoice date and a total amount. An invoice missing any of these is held and the supplier is asked for a corrected invoice.

### §4.2 Arithmetic
Line amounts must equal quantity × rate, lines must add up to the taxable value, and taxable value plus tax must equal the total, each within ₹1. An invoice that does not add up is returned to the supplier for correction. AP must not correct a supplier's arithmetic itself.

### §4.3 GST rate
The GST rate charged must match the rate for the supplier's goods or services (HSN/SAC). A different rate is held for a check with the tax team before payment.

### §4.4 Invoice date
Invoices dated in the future are returned to the supplier. Invoices older than 180 days are checked against payment history before payment, because input tax credit must be reversed if a supplier is not paid within 180 days of the invoice date.

### §4.5 Data capture confidence
Values captured automatically are only as good as the read. Where an amount or bank detail was read with low confidence, was corrected by the system, or two independent reads disagree, a person must confirm the value against the source document before payment. Corrections made by the system must be shown to the reviewer with the original reading. Identifiers proven by a check digit or by the supplier master (for example a GSTIN) do not need separate confirmation.

## §5 Purchase order matching

### §5.1 PO required
Every invoice must match a purchase order, except exempt categories (utilities, statutory dues, rent) which follow a separate procedure.

### §5.2 Invoices without a printed PO
If the invoice does not show a PO number, AP may identify the likely PO from the supplier's open orders, line items and amount. A PO identified this way must be confirmed with the requester before approval; it is never approved automatically.

### §5.3 Lines on the PO
Each invoice line must correspond to a line on the PO. Wording may differ between the supplier's invoice and the PO; the item, specification and model number must be the same. Lines not on the PO are held for Procurement to confirm they were ordered.

### §5.4 Price tolerance
Unit prices may differ from the PO by at most ±2% per line. A total that falls within tolerance does not excuse a line whose unit price is outside it: price changes hidden inside an unchanged total are a known leakage pattern. Price increases need written agreement from Procurement and a PO amendment, or the supplier issues a credit note.

### §5.5 PO balance and split invoicing
Suppliers may bill a PO across several invoices. The total invoiced against a PO, including earlier invoices, must not exceed the PO value or the ordered quantity (0% overrun). An invoice that would take the PO over is held; Procurement either raises a PO amendment or AP pays only the remaining balance.

## §6 Fraud and payment controls

### §6.1 Duplicate invoices
An invoice is a duplicate if the same supplier has already been paid an invoice with the same number, after removing punctuation, spaces and leading zeros (so "AFS/0042" and "AFS-42" are the same invoice). Duplicates are rejected and the supplier is told the invoice is already settled. An invoice from the same supplier for the same amount within 7 days of another is held so both can be compared. Instalments on a split PO with identical amounts are not duplicates if their numbers differ and the PO balance allows them.

### §6.2 Changes to bank details
Bank details are never changed on the strength of an invoice, an email or a phone call from the supplier. If an invoice shows bank details different from the supplier master, payment is stopped and Treasury calls the supplier on the phone number already on file — never a number on the invoice or in the email — and obtains written confirmation on letterhead signed by an authorised signatory with a cancelled cheque. The master is updated by Treasury under dual control before any payment is released. Changed bank details on an invoice are the most common payment-diversion fraud.

### §6.3 Segregation of duties
The person who approves an exception cannot be the person who updates the supplier master or releases the payment run.

## §7 Exceptions and approvals

### §7.1 Exception queues
Exceptions are routed to: Vendor follow-up (documents or corrections needed from the supplier), AP review (checks AP can resolve), Procurement (PO, price and quantity questions), Treasury verification (bank details), and Vendor onboarding (unknown suppliers).

### §7.2 Reviewer notes
Approving or rejecting an exception requires a written note saying what was checked and with whom. Notes become part of the audit trail and are used as precedent for similar cases.

### §7.3 Approval limits
Exceptions up to ₹5,00,000 may be approved by the AP lead; up to ₹25,00,000 by the Finance Controller; above that by the CFO.

### §7.4 Turnaround
Exceptions should be resolved within 3 working days. Supplier follow-ups are chased after 2 working days.

## §8 Payment

### §8.1 Terms
Invoices are paid on the supplier's agreed terms counted from the invoice date, in the weekly payment run. Early payment needs Finance Controller approval.

### §8.2 Payment run
Only invoices that are approved and posted to the AP ledger enter the payment run. Posting reduces the balance of the matched PO.

## §9 Records and audit

### §9.1 What is kept
For every invoice the original document, every extracted value, every check and its result, the decision, and any reviewer note are kept and can be replayed.

### §9.2 Retention
Records are kept for at least 72 months from the due date of the annual GST return for the year (CGST Act, section 36), and longer where a dispute or audit is open.
